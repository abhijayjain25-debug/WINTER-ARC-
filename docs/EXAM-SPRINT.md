# Exam sprint

Open **Exam sprint** in the sidebar, or **More → Exam sprint** on a phone. This is an optional JEE + CLAT/AILET plan; college and custom workspaces keep their existing roadmaps.

The default starts on the day you first use it and ends at your existing JEE planning target. Starting a block, saving settings, editing, or checking a goal saves the plan's start date with your current progress. A preview alone does not change your save.

## Start today

1. Press **Start now · 10 min**. It opens the exact task in the existing focus timer.
2. Work for those ten minutes. Finish and log actual study time. Continue the block after a normal break if you can.
3. Check the goal only after doing the work. Checkmarks do not award XP or mark a chapter mastered.
4. Use **Every week** to inspect the full calendar, and **Export full plan** to download all daily instructions as Markdown.

## The workload

The default assumes a recent baseline of roughly 1–3 focused hours per day. Ordinary days begin around two hours. Sessions grow from about 20 minutes to 30, 40 and 50 minutes, with a later workload around five to six focused hours. Full mocks are longer exam blocks. Sunday recall/reset days are lighter, except actual exam days. Breaks are additional to these focused-minute totals.

Use the 35-minute fallback on difficult days: a ten-minute start, fifteen minutes on one useful question set, then ten minutes recalling errors. Leave missed work in history; do not double tomorrow's load. During the weekly reset, choose at most two valuable repairs for spare blocks.

Take 5–10 minutes between blocks, a 25–40 minute meal or walk break after two or three blocks, and leave one evening period free. Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep. Normal rest and meals never need to be earned.

## Academic priorities

- Class 12 screenshot priorities come first, with short mole, bonding, organic chemistry and calculus prerequisites.
- Law uses a full-mock, analysis, targeted-repair and fresh-retest cycle. Current affairs comes from active recall of existing notes rather than endless new sources.
- After the law exams, Class 11 screenshot priorities receive dedicated work, followed by more JEE papers, analysis and error repair.
- Coverage is not mastery. Fresh checks on different days, independent attempts and mock trends determine which topics need another pass. Heavy chapters may need more repair blocks.

These chapter priorities do not guarantee questions or marks. Check topic boundaries against the official 2027 syllabus.

## Edit it

**Plan settings** changes the plan dates and pace. Choose 70% or 85% to hold a gentler pace, 100% for the default ramp, or 110% only when the workload is consistently manageable. Mock durations stay fixed. If you complete under 70% of blocks for three days, try a gentler week; move up after four steady days. This is a practical planning rule, not an automatic assessment of motivation.

Each block lets you edit its title, subject, duration and instructions. **Add block** adds your own work. In a block's editor, **Leave this block out** removes it from the scheduled load; **Restore omitted blocks** reverses that choice. Exam-day reminders are preserved.

Changing the horizon regenerates the dated curriculum without erasing study logs or XP. Shortening the horizon can reduce coverage and needs manual prioritisation. Existing block edits must remain within the date range.

## Saving and privacy

The plan, checkmarks, omitted blocks and edits are part of the existing Winter Arc save. They follow your chosen browser or device-file storage method and appear in normal JSON backups. Linked timer sessions record actual minutes. Starting the sprint does not replace your profile, name, companions, XP, old study logs or syllabus.

No account, network service, database, analytics or new dependency is needed. The monochrome view also pauses ambient snowfall rather than adding a new animation loop.
