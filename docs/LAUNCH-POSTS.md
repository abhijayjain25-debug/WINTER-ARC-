## Student community post

**Title: I built a free winter-themed study planner with evolving pixel companions — looking for students to test it**

I’m preparing for entrance exams and wanted something that makes coming back to studying feel a little more rewarding. So I built Winter Arc: a free, open-source study planner with a winter pixel-art basecamp.

It has focus timers, daily/lifetime study time, editable chapters, exam countdowns, mock analysis, and companions that evolve as you earn study XP. College students can import a PDF or Word syllabus, review its topics, and add them to their own course. There’s a blank profile option too, so it isn’t limited to JEE or law prep.

There’s no account or subscription. Your progress stays locally on your device; please keep backups. Phone layouts are in beta, and timer sounds can be limited when a phone is locked/backgrounded.

Try it: https://wintercarc.vercel.app/
Code: https://github.com/abhijayjain25-debug/WINTER-ARC-

I’d love feedback from students who try it for a week: what feels useful, what’s confusing, and what makes you stop using it? If it helps, a GitHub star would be appreciated.

## Developer / open-source community post

**Title: Winter Arc — an open-source, local-first gamified study planner built with vanilla JS**

I built Winter Arc while preparing for exams: a winter pixel-art planner with evolving companions, focus sessions, custom syllabuses, and a review queue.

It’s plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript with a static deployment and a small dependency-free local server. No backend database, accounts, analytics, or API keys. It supports browser storage plus export/restore, and user-selected JSON file autosave where the browser supports it. PDF.js and Mammoth are bundled and loaded only for document imports; documents are read on-device with an editable review step.

Live app: https://wintercarc.vercel.app/
Repository: https://github.com/abhijayjain25-debug/WINTER-ARC-

I’m especially looking for real-phone testing, awkward college syllabus samples (with private information removed), and accessibility feedback. Bug reports and focused PRs are welcome. This is a beta, not an APK/PWA, and device sync/background notifications are not implemented.

## First-week checklist

1. Invite 5–10 willing testers individually. Ask for actual use, not obligatory stars.
2. Ask after two days: could you set up, save, and finish a study session without help?
3. Ask after a week: did you return, what annoyed you, and what should be removed?
4. Keep feedback in Issues/Discussions. Prioritize saving bugs and blocked study flows before adding features.
5. Share one relevant, honest post per community where promotion is allowed. Read that community’s rules first; tailor the post and disclose that you made the app.
6. Share fixes with testers. Let stars be optional. Do not buy stars, exchange stars, or invent reviews/usage numbers.

Success measure: ten people voluntarily using it for a week. Download counts and GitHub stars alone do not show that.
